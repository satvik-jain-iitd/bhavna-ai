# ADR-013: Transcribe while talking; cut at pauses; one insert at release

Status: accepted, 2026-10-07

## Context
Measured on the owner's M1 (stats.jsonl): release-to-text grows with the dictation, about 0.2 s per
spoken second plus the decoder, because nothing starts until the key is released. 24 s → 5.5 s.
The owner asked for a proper, measured fix. Research (docs/research/stt_models.md rows 26 to 31):
Wispr Flow runs ASR during capture and inserts once after release with a p99 budget of 700 ms;
whisper.cpp's stream VAD mode decodes a segment per pause and passes the previous tokens as prompt;
re-decoding policies (LocalAgreement, WhisperKit Eager) cost double compute and 3 s latency; true
streaming models (FluidAudio Parakeet EOU) exist for English only.

## Decision
1. While the key is held, a `Chunker` cuts the audio at pauses: after at least 4 s of audio, a pause
   of 250 ms or more ends a chunk; with no pause by 12 s the cut lands on the quietest 20 ms of the
   last second. Chunks are trimmed to their speech bounds plus 200 ms. A burst under 250 ms is a
   click, not speech.
2. A worker thread transcribes chunks in order as they arrive, passing the previous chunk's last
   20 words as the decoder prompt for continuity. Errors on one chunk never stop the others.
3. At release, after the 300 ms post-roll, the last chunk is queued, the worker drains, the texts
   are joined, and the text is inserted once. Nothing appears on screen before release (ADR-010).
4. Every dictation records the full timing identity
   `release_to_text ≈ postroll + wait + final_asr + insert` in stats.jsonl and the full chunk table in
   the log json. `tools/report.py` reads them per length bucket.
5. Lean profile: all chunks through Apex. Full profile: language ID on the first chunk only.

## Trade-offs
- Context breaks at cuts: the prompt tail mitigates; punctuation at a cut may show as a period.
- A worker thread and a queue: more moving parts than one call after release.
- The RMS pause detector is simple: wrong cuts show up as `forced_cuts` or as text errors at
  boundaries, both visible in the log; a VAD model is the next step only if the log shows it.
- True streaming for English (S4.4) is better and is a follow-up for the full profile.

## Consequences
- Release-to-text targets: p50 < 1.0 s, p90 < 1.5 s for any dictation up to 60 s (H9).
- The decision table in the plan turns each dominant part of the identity into one lever.
